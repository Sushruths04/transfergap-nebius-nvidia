Human-to-robot transfer, diagnosed

Show a task once. Copy it onto a different body. Know why it fails.

A human arm has 7 degrees of freedom, a hand with fingers and a wrist that rolls. A UR arm has 6 joints and a two-finger gripper. This page documents exactly how the pipeline bridges that gap, what each stage costs, and what has and has not been verified.

02 · Hand to robot

How your movement becomes a robot movement

The live retargeting in live/arm_live.py is a calibrated, relative mapping, not a pose copy. Joint angles are never transferred between the human and the UR arm; only the hand's position and the pinch are.

Mapping (as implemented)

p_h  = hand position in the person's body frame
       (forward, left, up) from the hip line,
       relative to the right shoulder

calibrate:  p_0 = median(p_h) over the first seconds
            (arms down)  →  robot READY point r_0

each frame: r = clip( r_0 + g · (p_h − p_0) , workspace )
            r ← EMA(r)                 (smoothing)
            q = IK(r, tool pointing down)   6 DoF

gripper:    open  if  d(thumb,index) > a_open
            close if  d(thumb,index) < a_close   (hysteresis)

The gain g and the workspace alignment decide how faithfully “I put the apple there” becomes “the robot puts the apple there”. Fitting them from the object position (touch-the-object calibration) and checking the replay error is the next accuracy step.

05 · Object size

Does the copied style survive a bigger object?

Success vs cube edge (UR10e, 60 seeds, 95% Wilson band)

Reading

Without fixes the copy collapses to 0/60 at 7 cm on both UR arms. With the fixes UR10e holds 56/60 and UR5e 47/60 (near its 8.5 cm gripper opening); KAIROS (12.8 cm opening) stays at 60/60. The fixes were tuned at 4 cm only. Exploratory: one clip, one cube shape, sizes chosen by us.

Idea from HOI-Retarget’s object-scale experiments; data in results/object_scale_v1.

08 · Limits

Read before quoting a number

  • Simulation only; one clip and one person; shoulder width 0.44 m is derived, not measured.
  • Perception and calibration error sizes are assumed values.
  • Joint angles are not copied; the tool orientation is fixed; the gripper uses only the pinch.
  • The G1’s 27% on the long-lived pod versus 77% in the image is unexplained.
  • Single-seed videos are illustrations; the 60-seed counts are the evidence.
